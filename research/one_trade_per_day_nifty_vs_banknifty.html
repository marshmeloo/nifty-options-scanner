One Trade a Day: NIFTY and Bank Nifty Side by Side

What the breakeven@0.5R momentum strategy does when it takes only the first qualifying signal of each session, at one lot — the smallest, lowest-intensity version of the system, and the one that answers "how little capital does this actually need?"

Prepared 2026-08-15 Condition max 1 new trade/day · 1 lot · breakeven@0.5R NIFTY 1,179 trades · Bank Nifty 1,040 trades Backtest only — not deployed
Executive Summary

The live system takes every signal that clears its conviction bar — around 7 trades a day. This note asks what happens under a hard cap of one trade per session: the first qualifying signal chronologically, no cherry-picking the day's best setup, everything else skipped. Position size is one lot, which is what MAX_LOTS_PER_TRADE = 1 already produces live.

Both indices stay profitable and both become dramatically calmer. NIFTY returns +₹6,91,645 with a maximum drawdown of just ₹12,609; Bank Nifty returns +₹8,27,291 with a ₹34,901 drawdown. Compared with the unlimited-trade versions, NIFTY gives up 81% of its profit to shed 85% of its drawdown.

The capital answer: because a long option requires the full premium and no margin, and because these trades never overlap, the working capital needed is small — roughly ₹22,000 for NIFTY and ₹52,000 for Bank Nifty to survive both the largest premium and the deepest drawdown. That produces enormous percentage returns, which §5 argues is the least meaningful way to read these results.

01 — What "One Trade a Day" Means Here

The cap is applied inside the backtest engine as max_trades_per_day = 1. Once a day's first signal clears every gate — conviction bar, bias gate, risk checks — the rest of that session is skipped entirely, regardless of what appears later.

This matters methodologically: the rule takes the chronologically first qualifying setup, not the highest-scoring one. Picking the day's best setup would require knowing how the day turned out, which is look-ahead bias. Everything below is what a live operator could actually have done.

Both runs are fresh from-scratch scanner replays with the full real cost stack — statutory charges plus measured bid-ask spread on both legs — and the breakeven@0.5R exit that is live in production.

03 — How Much Capital This Actually Needs

Buying a long option requires the full premium up front — no margin, no leverage. And because the cap allows only one position at a time, trades never overlap. So the capital question reduces to: enough to pay the largest premium you will face, while never being driven negative by a losing streak first.

Two figures are worth separating, because they answer different questions:

  • Minimum starting capital — walking the real trade sequence from day one, the smallest balance that never goes negative. This is a backtest-perfect floor: it assumes you start on exactly this date and every fill lands as modeled.
  • Safe working capital — the largest single premium plus the deepest drawdown. This survives hitting the worst stretch at the worst time, rather than relying on having banked a cushion first.

Why the floor is lower than it looks, and why not to use it
NIFTY's ₹7,974 floor is set by the very first trade in the series (2020-08-04), not by the worst drawdown — at that point there is no profit cushion at all. By the time the ₹12,609 drawdown arrives in 2024, the account has banked far more than that. The floor therefore depends entirely on starting at a favourable moment. Use the safe-capital figure instead: it holds regardless of when you begin.

Bank Nifty needs roughly 2.3× the capital of NIFTY for this strategy, driven by both a higher premium band (₹300–800 versus NIFTY's far cheaper contracts) and a drawdown nearly 3× as deep.

05 — What the Cap Costs, and Why % Return Misleads

Against the unlimited-trade version of the same strategy, capping at one trade a day is a large sacrifice of profit for a large reduction in pain:

NIFTYUnlimited trades/dayOne trade/day
Trades10,6601,179
Net P&L+₹36,79,201+₹6,91,645
Max drawdown₹83,764₹12,609
Win rate (2R target reached)23.5%29.4%
Profit per rupee of drawdown44 : 155 : 1

The cap keeps 19% of the profit while eliminating 85% of the drawdown — and the win rate actually improves from 23.5% to 29.4%, which is consistent with the first clean signal of a session being better than the marginal fifth or sixth one. On a profit-per-unit-of-drawdown basis the capped version is the better strategy; on absolute profit it is far behind.

Do not read the percentage return
Dividing ₹6,91,645 by ₹22,355 of working capital gives roughly 3,000%, and Bank Nifty's gives roughly 1,600%. These numbers are arithmetically correct and practically meaningless. Position size here is fixed at one lot regardless of account size, so the "return %" is just an artifact of choosing the smallest capital that survives — it does not compound, does not scale, and would not survive doubling the account (which would simply sit idle). Read the absolute rupee figures and the drawdown; treat the percentage as a footnote about capital efficiency, not a rate of return.

06 — Limitations

01

Backtest only — this configuration is not deployed

The live processes take every qualifying signal, not one a day. Everything here is a counterfactual replay of what a one-a-day rule would have produced; no live trade has ever run under it.

02

No real-money execution, and no order-entry delay modeled

Nothing in this system has ever placed a real broker order. Every figure assumes a fill at exactly the price that existed when the signal fired; a real order is punched in seconds later and fills elsewhere. That gap is unmodeled and asymmetric — a delayed exit fills systematically worse, because stops and breakeven exits fire precisely when price is moving against the position.

03

Personal income tax

All figures are pre-tax. F&O profit is taxed as non-speculative business income at your own slab rate and cannot be generically modeled.

04

Concentration rises when trades are scarce

With roughly nine times fewer trades, single trades matter more: top-3 concentration rises from 0.76% to 3.86% (NIFTY) and to 4.48% (Bank Nifty). Still far from outlier-dependent, but no longer negligible.

05

The breakeven exit fill assumption

Trades closed by the rule are modeled as exiting at ~entry price while still paying full statutory and spread cost. Real stop fills on fast reversals may differ.

06

Different windows, so the two indices are not perfectly comparable

NIFTY covers 2020-08 onward (73 months), Bank Nifty 2021-08 onward (61 months). Bank Nifty never sees the 2020–21 period. Absolute totals are not like-for-like.
